Federated Evolutionary Neural Architecture Search with FedAvg:
Validation of Client Selection and Multiobjective Search

EvoFederated Research Group

Three-seed Fashion-MNIST replication | 8 clients | Dirichlet α = 0.5

Abstract. We study evolutionary neural architecture search in which every candidate is trained as one global model with FedAvg. Search fitness uses the mean and worst-client validation accuracy, while test data is reserved for final re-training. The campaign compares NSGA-II, random search, NSGA-III, MOEA/D, a fixed MLP, and partial client participation across three paired seeds. Full participation requires 448 local trainings per search; four-client strategies require 224. MOEA/D reaches the highest mean test accuracy among matched-budget optimizers (0.758), while probabilistic dissimilarity is the strongest partial method by mean accuracy (0.732). The 50% search-cost reduction is accompanied by lower mean accuracy for every partial strategy, so the results do not establish selection superiority.

Question

Can evolutionary NAS evaluate global FedAvg models under a controlled client budget while preserving mean and worst-client quality in non-IID federated data?

Protocol

  • Fashion-MNIST, 8 clients, Dirichlet concentration α = 0.5.
  • MLP genomes with up to 2 hidden layers and widths {16, 32, 64}.
  • Adam, learning rate 10-3, batch size 128, one local epoch and one FedAvg round.
  • Population 8 and 7 generations, yielding 56 architecture evaluations.
  • Search uses validation only; final evaluation re-trains with full participation on test.
  • Paired seeds 42, 123, and 999; uncertainty intervals are descriptive.

Algorithms

The inner fitness is computed from one global model. The same aggregated state is evaluated on every validation client.

FEDAVG-FIT(A, schedule)
theta <- deterministic_initialize(A)
for each round r:
    for client i in schedule[r]:
        model <- instantiate(A); load_global_state(model, theta)
        local_train(model, train[i])
        updates[i] <- model_state(model)
    theta <- weighted_average(updates, n_train[i])
scores <- evaluate(theta, validation[i]) for every client i
return (-mean(scores), -min(scores)), theta

Similarity and dissimilarity schedules are obtained from a common one-epoch probe. Four pair representatives alternate across generations and rounds, with one representative from each pair in every search evaluation. Probabilistic pairing records tau and the selected pairs.

FEDERATED-NAS(protocol, strategy, optimizer)
   clients <- partition data into disjoint train/validation/test subsets
schedule <- characterize clients and build participant schedule
population <- initialize genomes
for generation g in 1..G:
    evaluate every genome with FEDAVG-FIT on validation
    population <- evolutionary_step(population, fitness, optimizer)
top <- select by validation mean, then worst client
retrain top with full participation; evaluate test once
   record schedules, history, costs, and final metrics

Analysis plan

The primary outcomes are mean and minimum test accuracy across clients. Macro-F1 is secondary. Search cost is reported as local client trainings and elapsed CPU time. With three paired seeds, all comparisons and intervals are descriptive rather than confirmatory. The independent replication unit is the paired seed and partition.

Interpretation

Four-client participation reduced search local trainings by 50%. Probabilistic dissimilarity had the highest mean accuracy among partial conditions, while deterministic dissimilarity had the highest minimum-client accuracy. The three-seed design cannot distinguish a selection effect from stochastic optimization or partition-specific behavior. Hypervolumes are not pooled across optimizer families because NSGA-II has two objectives whereas NSGA-III and MOEA/D have one objective per client.

Evidence status. This is a three-seed replication, not a definitive statistical campaign. The next step is alpha sensitivity and tau/probe-length ablations only after verifying that schedules change.
